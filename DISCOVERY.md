# Discovery summary

## Problem summary

Nadia Comeau, Sandbar Scheduling's CEO, spends about $9,000 on each of four trade shows a year. After a show, she cannot reliably tell whether Sandbar followed up with everyone it spoke to, how many demos or trials came from the show, or whether one show performs better than another.

The problem is not simply that the team writes slowly. Information about one conversation is split across badge scans, business cards, voice notes, Owen's flight-home list and Nadia's memory. Erin then tries to match those records by hand. Owen sends from his own email while Erin sends from the shared inbox, so neither has a complete view. Cards arrive late, people can appear more than once, and nobody owns the whole process from the booth conversation to the completed follow-up.

## Who has the problem

- **Nadia Comeau, CEO:** pays for the shows and needs evidence that Sandbar followed up properly and that the shows produce useful results.
- **Owen Gallant, sales:** needs a complete phone-friendly list of his conversations, with short messages and calls he can act on while travelling.
- **Erin Raman, operations:** spends hours typing, matching and checking records and needs one clear review process that does not require code.

Nadia is the person ultimately accountable because she approves the shows, but all three people experience part of the problem.

## What they experience

- Toronto had roughly 80 real conversations, but Erin's recount found that only 31 people received any follow-up.
- People who clearly asked for a demo, pricing, a trial, a document or a later call could still receive nothing.
- The same person could receive two emails because their badge, card and informal note were not recognized as one person.
- Drive-bys, people who did not give contact permission and people who asked not to be contacted must not receive a sales message.
- Erin spends about 10 hours after each show typing cards and matching records by hand.
- Owen works from his phone while travelling and will return to his own list if the result misses someone, sounds robotic or requires several apps.
- Nadia cannot compare shows because the team has never produced the same complete results for each one.

## The impact

- Each show costs about **$9,000**, before counting staff time.
- Sandbar attends **four two-day shows each year**, so the direct annual show spend is about **$36,000**.
- Nadia cannot confidently decide which show to stop attending.
- Missed promises can lose clinics that may stay for years. Nadia said one or two new clinics can pay for a show.
- Duplicate or unwanted emails could damage trust or create a complaint.
- Erin's work on show records pushes aside invoices and the shared inbox.

## How often it happens

This happens after every show: four times a year. Toronto is the largest and the only show the team has fully recounted, but Nadia, Owen and Erin described the same kind of problem after Halifax and Montreal.

## Root cause

The root cause is a broken handoff, not a lack of effort.

1. Conversation details are spread across several files and people's memories.
2. There is no single list containing every real conversation and the next action.
3. Nobody owns the whole week after a show.
4. Erin, Owen and Nadia work at different times and in different places.
5. Sent emails, bookings and replies are not visible to everyone, causing missed or duplicate follow-up.
6. Matching people across records is slow and uncertain, especially when names or emails differ.
7. There was no consistent day-30 report for comparing results.

## The outcome they want

Immediately after a show, every real conversation should appear once with:

- the person and clinic;
- where the information came from;
- why records were combined;
- who owns the conversation;
- what was requested or promised;
- whether contact is allowed;
- a safe draft, a call task, or a clear reason that no message should be prepared.

The notes should be ready by the Thursday after the team lands. A person must review every message; nothing sends automatically.

At day 30, Nadia wants every real conversation placed in one of four results:

1. demo booked;
2. trial started;
3. contacted, but no demo or trial;
4. not contacted.

Day 30 grades Sandbar's follow-up. It does not yet prove revenue or decide which show to cancel. Nadia wants several shows compared using the same totals, followed later by the number of paying clinics.

## Important examples from the interviews

- **Hélène:** Nadia promised a French follow-up call after Thanksgiving. She needs a call reminder, not a generic sales message.
- **The Saint John physiotherapist:** clearly requested a demo but fell between Erin's card stack and Owen's flight-home list.
- **Jen from Bayview:** received two emails because her card, badge and informal list entry were not matched correctly and Nadia's booking was not visible to Owen.
- **Drive-by scans:** people who only entered a draw or collected something are not real conversations and must not receive drafts.
