# Follow-up 2 (10 min): Owen Gallant, sales lead

Sandbar Scheduling · simulated client · Greyson Malabanan
Started 2026-11-05T00:49:47 · ended 2026-11-05T00:56:19

What I wanted to find out: Understand how Owen would actually use the shared work queue, drafts and call reminders from his phone, and what correction process he would complete consistently.

**Greyson:** After Erin checks the matches, you would receive on your phone one list where each person has an owner, the reason they are there, a draft or promised call, a due date and an open or done status. Walk me through what you would actually do from the moment that list appears.

**Owen:** Hotel room, nine at night, sandwich, phone. I scroll straight to my people who asked for something, read the draft, fix a word, type in the price if it's a pricing one, and hit send. If marking it done isn't automatic or one tap, I'll forget, I'm telling you now. The calls I'd do the next day between clinic visits, so I'd want those to pop up as a reminder with the one thing to mention, not sitting in some list I have to remember to open.

The bit I'd actually be nervous about is the lines from my plane list it couldn't place. Those I'd sort myself, but only if they're right there on the same list, not in another app.

**Greyson:** Here are two drafts from the current version:

Great to meet you in Toronto.
You mentioned that you use Bookstead.
You wanted a demo, so here is the link to choose a time: [[DEMO LINK]].
Owen

Great to meet you in Toronto.
You mentioned that you use Bookstead.
You asked for pricing: [[OWEN: ADD PRICE]].
Owen

What would you change before sending these, and why?

**Owen:** They're close, but they read like a form someone filled in. Nobody types "you mentioned that you use Bookstead" to a person. I'd say something like "you said Bookstead's been a pain for reminders", which is the one thing we actually talked about. If there's no real detail to use, I'd rather it skip that line than just name their software back to them.

On the demo one, say it's with Nadia and give them a backup, like "or I'll give you a call Thursday." People ignore links, but they don't ignore a call. The pricing one is fine with the gap for the price, but it needs one more line after the number, like "happy to walk you through it, I'll call you next week." Otherwise I've just sent a number and walked away.

**Greyson:** The current prototype does not connect to your real inbox or calendar, so it cannot automatically detect that you sent an email or create a phone notification. Within that limit, what is the smallest action you would realistically take on your phone to mark an email or call complete, and what would you need to see afterward to trust that Erin can also see it?

**Owen:** One tap, right next to the person, the second I hit send. Like a checkbox on the same screen as the draft. If I have to open something else, find the person again and pick from a menu, it won't happen. For calls, same tap once I hang up. Maybe one optional line like "call back Friday" if I'm feeling generous, but don't make me fill it in.

Afterward I'd want to see it flip to done with the date next to it, and know it's the same list Erin opens, not my own copy. If it's one list and I can see my tick stuck there, I'll trust that she can see it too. You'd have to ask Erin what she'd need to check on her end though.

**Greyson:** If the list gives someone the wrong next step, combines two different people or keeps one person as two records, what would you realistically do from your phone so the problem gets corrected without disappearing into another message?

**Owen:** Same as the tick, really. A "wrong" tap next to the person, and a little box where I type three words like "same as Jen" or "wants a call, not demo" or "two people." That stays on the list with my name and the date on it, so Erin sees it on her Tuesday. I'm not writing her an email about it and I'm not opening a spreadsheet in a hotel.

What I'd want after that is for that person's draft to stop being "ready" until it's fixed, so I don't send the wrong thing by accident at eleven at night. Erin's the one who'd actually fix the match, so how she wants the fix written down is her call, not mine.

**Greyson:** Consider three people: Derek represents six clinics and requested a security document, Rachel has one clinic already using Clinivo but requested nothing, and Vanessa represents five clinics and requested information. Which would you open first, and what makes that order useful to you?

**Owen:** Derek. Six clinics and he asked for a real thing, so I send the one-pager and call him. Fair warning, Nadia and I marked him warm in Erin's recount, and she doesn't count an IT one-pager as asking for a demo. From my seat he's first anyway.

After that, honestly, I'd open Vanessa because five clinics is five deals, and "send me info" is how people get rid of you, so it's her size that matters, not what she asked for. Nadia would open Rachel first because she's on Clinivo, and that's the argument we have every show. I can't place Rachel from memory either, so check it against how we actually marked them in the recount. The order matters less to me than all three having a draft ready.

**Greyson:** What would make you stop using this shared list and return to your own plane list or writing drafts yourself?

**Owen:** If it misses someone I know I talked to. The first time I go looking for someone who's on my plane list and they're not there, I'm back to the list on my desk. Same if a draft goes to the wrong person, or something personal slips into one, even once.

The other ways to lose me are slower. The drafts sound like a robot so I'm rewriting all of them, or sending means copying and pasting between three apps, or it's not ready by my road week. Any of those and I'll give up by the second night, and the plane list goes back to sitting on my desk making me feel guilty.

**Greyson:** Let me check my understanding. You would use one phone-friendly shared queue where people who asked for something appear first, the draft uses the actual professional detail discussed, calls include the one thing to mention, and unplaced plane-list notes remain visible. You need one-tap Done and Wrong actions that leave your name and date for Erin. Missing someone, assigning a draft to the wrong person or including a personal detail would immediately break your trust. Robotic drafts, multiple applications or late preparation would make you abandon it after a night or two. What have I misunderstood or left out?

**Owen:** Pretty much, yeah. Two things. When I tap Wrong, that draft has to stop showing as ready until Erin fixes it, or I'll send it anyway at eleven at night. And anyone I already wrote to or who already booked a demo gets no draft, just a heads-up to me, because right now nobody tells me about demos.

Also, it's only my people on my phone. Nadia's go to her, French ones included. Right, I'm pulling into the clinic and my sandwich went cold somewhere around Derek, so I've got to run. Good luck with it.

**Greyson:** This project cannot connect directly to your real inbox or send email for you. Starting from a draft on your phone, what is the smallest handoff into your email app that you would tolerate without abandoning the process?

**Owen:** Quick one, I'm in the parking lot. One tap that opens my own email with the address, subject and draft already filled in, so all I do is fix a word, add the price if there is one, and hit send. If I have to copy the address, then the subject, then the body, I'm done by the second night, I'm telling you now.

Then I come back and tap Done. That's my limit, one tap out and one tap back. Gotta go, good luck.

**Greyson:** Thanks, Owen. I’ll use a one-tap link that opens your own email app with the recipient, subject and body filled in, while leaving the final edit and Send action to you. Returning to the shared queue and marking Done will remain one tap.

**Owen:** Perfect, that's exactly it. If it really is that simple, I'll actually use it. Talk soon.
